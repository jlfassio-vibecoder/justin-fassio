## **Public Database Architecture and Programmatic Extraction Framework**

To fully enrich the contact profiles of the targeted businesses, a multi-layered extraction protocol must be applied to public databases. ### **The Oregon Secretary of State Business Registry Infrastructure**

The Oregon SOS Corporation Division maintains the definitive legal repository for all domestic and foreign business entities operating within the state, encompassing over 1.5 million active entities3. This registry houses critical data points including the entity's exact legal name, assumed business names (DBAs), Registry Number, formation date, and the identity and physical address of the Registered Agent3.  
Extracting accurate contact data from this portal requires navigating specific architectural and terminological nuances unique to Oregon. For instance, the primary entity identification number is officially classified as the "Registry Number," and the incorporation date is termed the "Registry Date"3. Furthermore, the primary "Active Business Search" portal exclusively returns entities currently in good standing, automatically filtering out administratively dissolved, withdrawn, or inactive organizations3. If a business fails to comply with annual report filings, it is marked as "Administratively Dissolved," whereas a business that voluntarily ceases operations is marked "Inactive" or "Dissolved"7.  
For programmatic data extraction and scraping at scale, the database can be accessed via application programming interfaces (APIs), such as the Apify NexGenData Oregon Business Registry Scraper and the Crawler Bros Oregon SOS Scraper5. These API architectures facilitate automated HTTP POST requests using unique authentication tokens, allowing data analysts to input arrays of entity names via JSON and retrieve standardized outputs containing the official Department of State (DOS) record, county assignments, and registered officers5. The OpenAPI specification—a standard for designing and describing RESTful APIs—is heavily utilized here to define the API structure and data formats in a machine-readable way, which is vital for seamless AI integration and high-volume data retrieval5.  
The state also provides public datasets via the Socrata OData protocol, allowing access to bulk files such as the "Active Businesses \- County Data" (483,000 rows) and "Active Nonprofit Corporations" (182,000 rows)12. This programmatic access is vital for verifying the legal standing of retail targets and cross-referencing assumed business names with their ultimate corporate ownership structures, ensuring wholesale contracts are executed with the correct legal entity14.

### **Industry-Specific Licensing and Operational Directories**

Beyond legal registries, operational licensing databases provide high-confidence verification regarding a retail business's current commercial activity. The Oregon Department of Fish and Wildlife (ODFW) maintains a continuously updated, publicly accessible directory of authorized license agents1. Because ODFW agents must maintain active state contracts, utilize specific retail point-of-sale systems, and process electronic funds transfers to remit state fees, a retailer's presence on the ODFW active agent list acts as an authoritative, real-time verification of operational status, physical location, and direct dial telephone numbers2.  
Similarly, the Oregon State Marine Board (OSMB) publishes data on certified Clean Marinas and boating facilities, providing verified geographical and operational data for marine supply operations and chandleries across the state1. The Oregon Golf Association (OGA) also maintains a comprehensive club and course directory18. The OGA specifically directs public and member inquiries to the facility's pro shop, thereby serving as a robust primary discovery layer for golf retail contacts, mapping facilities across the Portland Metro, Salem area, Central Oregon, and Southern Oregon regions19.

The comprehensive extraction and structuring of this contact data yield several third-order insights that dictate how commercial entities should approach the Oregon specialty retail market.
