# Oregon Big Wheel Look-Alike Store Research

## Executive summary

I treated **Big Wheel General Store** in Bandon as the seed account and used the profile in the uploaded research as the governing definition of a look-alike: an independent or small-regional destination retailer with meaningful apparel, gifts/souvenirs, broad-category merchandising, tourist or recreation traffic, men's/unisex relevance, and enough assortment breadth to create multi-line potential for brands such as Old Guys Rule, Big Fish Gear, Eagle Peak, and Go Hammock.

The research produced **270 named Oregon prospects** across 46 cities and 23 counties, plus Big Wheel itself as the seed-reference record. Of those prospects:

| Confidence | Count |
| ---------- | ----: |
| High       |   163 |
| Medium     |   100 |
| Low        |     7 |

The scoring model identified **31 Tier A/A+** prospects (1 A+ and 30 Tier A).

I did not manufacture a 500-row list simply to hit a requested number. There is a much larger raw retail universe: a commercial directory currently reports roughly 1,827 Oregon gift shops (about 1,329 single-owner), while Oregon's official Active Businesses dataset contains approximately 1.56 million business-related records (updated July 2026). [[1]](#works-cited) But the seed definition is substantially narrower than “gift shop,” and the source material explicitly cautions against dumping hundreds of loosely related gift stores into the CRM merely to increase volume.

Accordingly, **270 is the defensible research set from this pass, not 500**. Reaching 500 while retaining address, phone, website/email/contact, and verification standards would require loosening the Big Wheel similarity threshold or using a paid commercial lead database. That distinction matters because the purpose is sales productivity, not list size.

## Seed model and qualification logic

The seed is best understood not as “a general store that sells fudge,” but as a **Destination General Store / Tourist Mercantile**. The uploaded profile emphasizes:

- Tourist and outdoor-recreation traffic
- T-shirts, sweatshirts, hats, and casual apparel as a genuine department
- Men's/unisex relevance
- Gifts and souvenirs
- Broad assortment and local/regional product relevance
- Recreational browsing and physical merchandising capacity
- Accessible independent or small-chain buyer

Discovery therefore expanded beyond businesses literally named “General Store” to include mercantiles, trading companies, outfitters, surf shops, western stores, resort shops, destination gift stores, maker marketplaces, lifestyle boutiques, specialty food/gift hybrids, fishing stores, country stores, and locally oriented multi-category shops.

### Tier definitions (retained from seed model)

| Tier                   | Score range |
| ---------------------- | ----------- |
| A+                     | 85–100      |
| A                      | 70–84       |
| B                      | 55–69       |
| C                      | 40–54       |
| Disqualify / very weak | &lt; 40     |

The automatic score is sales prioritization, not a prediction of purchasing volume. A buyer with modest similarity but strong relationship, route fit, or category opening can still outperform a higher-scoring account.

### Examples validating non–“general store” analogues

- **The Ruddy Duck** (Hood River) — ~3,000 sq ft; clothing, shoes, accessories, gifts, textiles, home goods [[2]](#works-cited)
- **Doug's Sports** — outdoor equipment + clothing in a destination recreation market
- **The Wy'East Store** (Timberline) — winter apparel, logo wear, souvenirs
- Coast: **Safari Town Surf Shop** (Lincoln City, since 1989); **By the Sea Treasures** (Bandon); **The Market Place** (Brookings) [[3]](#works-cited)

## Research methodology and verification

Layered discovery-and-verification (not a single directory):

1. **Tourism / destination-retail sources** prioritized (Travel Oregon and local DMO listings) for assortment, visitor orientation, years in operation, and location.
2. **Oregon Secretary of State / Open Data** as the authoritative identity and location framework (Active Businesses updated July 28, 2026; county-enriched views derived from city/ZIP). [[4]](#works-cited)
3. **Commercial directory data** used only for discovery and market sizing — not as principal evidence for High-confidence accounts. [[5]](#works-cited)

Verification protocol: use **“unspecified”** rather than inferred/invented data when a required field is not exposed (especially websites, public emails, named owners/managers, exact square footage).

County may be inferred from researched city and should be labeled as such in verification notes. [[6]](#works-cited)

The low website/email completion rate is why this is a **researched prospect universe**, not a fully enriched CRM import. Address/phone and retail-model qualification are relatively strong; direct buyer-contact enrichment remains incomplete.

## Geographic and category distribution

The 270-prospect dataset is weighted toward tourist towns, recreation destinations, historic shopping districts, and coastal markets — not Oregon population share. Cities such as Bandon, Brookings, Joseph, Hood River, Lincoln City, Sisters, and Prineville appear more prominently than population would predict.

A large share of the best Big Wheel prospects do not self-identify as general stores. Apparel-heavy Western stores, surf shops, resort stores, and lifestyle retailers may be economically closer to Big Wheel than a literal “general store” that is primarily convenience grocery.

Examples: The Ruddy Duck (lifestyle); Safari Town and Cleanline (surf); Wy'East (resort); Pendleton Round-Up store (Western/souvenir). [[42]](#works-cited)

Portland was intentionally underweighted: SmartScraper counts ~550 gift shops in Portland alone, but a generic urban gift boutique does not automatically match Big Wheel economics. [[43]](#works-cited)

### Route clusters (sales interpretation)

1. **Oregon Coast** — Astoria → Seaside → Cannon Beach → Manzanita → Tillamook → Lincoln City → Newport → Florence → Coos Bay/Bandon → Gold Beach → Brookings. Especially Bandon–Brookings: By the Sea Treasures, Port O' Call, The Market Place, a.ell, Semi Aquatic, River's End Collective. [[47]](#works-cited)
2. **Central Oregon** — Bend → Redmond → Sisters → Sunriver → Prineville (Western/outdoor lifestyle; Sunriver outfitters). [[48]](#works-cited)
3. **Columbia Gorge / Mount Hood** — Hood River → The Dalles → Government Camp (Ruddy Duck, Doug's Sports, Gorge Fly Shop, Big Winds, Gorge Dog, Made in the Gorge; Wy'East). [[49]](#works-cited) [[50]](#works-cited)
4. **Southern Oregon** — Ashland → Jacksonville → Grants Pass → Medford (Paddington Station; Red Canary Mercantile; Gallop 'n Glitz). [[51]](#works-cited)
5. **Eastern Oregon** — Pendleton → Baker City → La Grande → Joseph (Round-Up, Woolen Mills, Hamley, toZion, Copper Creek Mercantile; A Piece of Pendleton). [[52]](#works-cited)

### Practical selling tiers

- **31 A/A+** — immediate route-building core
- **93 Tier B** — second layer for assortment depth, men's relevance, buyer accessibility
- **C tier** — territory density / single-line fits, not equal-priority appointments

## Deliverables, data quality, and limitations

### Expected CSV fields (full 270-row export)

`rank`, `store_name`, `street_address`, `city`, `county`, `zip`, `phone`, `website_url`, `public_contact_email_or_form`, `owner_manager_contact`, `store_category`, `broad_category`, `estimated_store_size`, `seed_similarity_score`, `tier`, `confidence`, `verification_status`, `match_rationale`, `source_urls`, `verification_notes`, `record_status`, `researched_as_of`

### Limitations

1. **500-record target** — larger raw universe exists; only 270 retained under seed logic. [[5]](#works-cited) [[53]](#works-cited)
2. **Contact enrichment** — address/phone stronger than website, buyer email, named owners. Missing fields marked unspecified (no guessed emails).
3. **Store size** rarely public; preserved where credible (Ruddy Duck ~3k, Top Drawer ~6k, Paddington Station &gt;8k, Portland evo ~11k). [[54]](#works-cited)
4. **Look-alike** is commercial similarity, not a government classification. High confidence ≠ A-tier fit automatically.

### Conclusion

Big Wheel represents a repeatable Oregon retail archetype. Highest-probability analogues cluster where destination shopping, outdoor recreation, apparel, and gift purchasing overlap — especially on the Oregon Coast.
